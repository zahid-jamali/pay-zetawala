### 4.3 Standard CGPA Calculation Formula & Repeated Course Handling
1. **Semester GPA Formula:**
   $$\text{GPA} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (\text{Credit Units}_i \times \text{Grade Point}_i)}{\sum_{i=1}^n \text{Credit Units}_i}$$
2. **Cumulative CGPA Formula:**
   $$\text{CGPA} = \frac{\sum_{\text{all semesters}} \text{Quality Points}}{\sum_{\text{all semesters}} \text{Credit Units}}$$
3. **Repeated / Carryover Courses Rule:**
   Under standard NUC and NBTE regulatory guidelines, both the initial course attempt (including the F grade of 0 quality points and its credit units) and the repeated course attempt (with the newly earned grade point and credit units) remain on the transcript and are cumulated into the CGPA denominator and numerator. An institutional senate may enact specific local provisions (e.g. grade replacement or capping), but the national regulatory default is cumulative inclusion without deletion of past failed units.

## 5. Conflicts Section

1. **Philippines: SSS Salary Loan Interest Rate (8% vs. 10%)**
   - *Conflict:* Prior guidance and older circulars (Circular No. 2013-008 and 2017 circulars) specified a flat 10.0% interest per annum on diminishing principal balance.
   - *Resolution & Verification:* SSS Circular No. 2025-004 (issued June 2025 under SSC Res. No. 179-s.2025) officially reduced the interest rate to **8.0% per annum** on diminishing balance for members with good credit standing / initial applications. The 10.0% rate is retained only for borrowers who previously availed of a penalty condonation program within the past five years. Both rules are implemented in the data rules.

2. **Kenya: Safaricom M-PESA Unregistered User Transfers**
   - *Conflict:* Historically, Safaricom published a separate column for "Transfer to Unregistered Users" with significantly higher fees.
   - *Resolution & Verification:* Following Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) mobile money interoperability directives, Safaricom merged and harmonized all domestic mobile money transfers into "Transfer to M-PESA Users" and "Transfer to Other Network Registered Mobile Money Users" with identical tariffs. Unregistered cash vouchers have been eliminated from the active tariff sheet.

3. **Nigeria: University Grade "E" and Pass Degree Abolition**
   - *Conflict:* The traditional BMAS scale contains Grade E (40–44%, 1 GP) and a "Pass" classification (1.00–1.49 CGPA). In 2013–2015, NUC officially abolished the Pass degree, requiring a minimum CGPA of 1.50 to graduate. Some universities eliminated Grade E (setting 0–44% as F), while others preserved Grade E but denied graduation to anyone below 1.50 CGPA.
   - *Resolution & Verification:* Verified via official NUC announcement (`https://www.nuc.edu.ng/stakeholders-review-grading-system-in-nigerian-universities/`). The data model supports both the standard 6-tier (A–F) and the 5-tier (E-omitted) university systems.

4. **Uganda: Mobile Money Withdrawal Tax in Operator Tables**
   - *Conflict:* Some secondary sources state that operator withdrawal fees include the 0.5% government tax.
   - *Resolution & Verification:* The official MTN Uganda tariff table (`https://www.mtn.co.ug/tariffs/mobile-money-tariffs/`) clearly tabulates the operator Agent Withdraw Fee (e.g., 700 UGX for 10,000 UGX) alongside a separate statutory column "Withdraw Tax (0.5%)" (e.g., 50 UGX). MTN notes that statutory tax is automatically deducted from the wallet alongside the operator withdrawal charge.
